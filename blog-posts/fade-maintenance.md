# How Often Should You Get a Fade? A Barber's Guide to Fade Maintenance

A fresh fade is one of the cleanest looks you can walk out of a barbershop with. The gradients are tight, the line-up is crisp, and everything just *sits right*. Then two weeks go by, and suddenly the blend isn't blending anymore.

So how do you keep a fade looking good between visits — and how often should you actually be coming back?

## The General Rule: Every 2–4 Weeks

For most guys, the sweet spot for fade maintenance is **every 2 to 4 weeks**. That's a pretty wide window, and where you fall in it depends on a few factors:

**How tight is your fade?** A high skin fade or a bald fade drops down to the skin, which means any regrowth is immediately visible. If you're rocking something like that, you're probably looking at 2 weeks before it starts to lose its shape. A mid or low fade with more length on top gives you a bit more breathing room — closer to 3–4 weeks before it looks overgrown.

**How fast does your hair grow?** The average person's hair grows about half an inch per month, but this varies. If your hair grows fast, you'll feel the fade disappearing sooner. Some guys can tell by week two. Others can stretch to a full month.

**How sharp is your line-up?** The line-up around your temples and neckline is often the first thing to look messy. Even if the fade itself still looks decent, a grown-out hairline can make the whole cut look unkempt. This is a good way to gauge when you need to come back in.

## Signs It's Time for a Touch-Up

You don't always need to count the days. Here's what to look for:

- The skin or low-fade section has noticeable stubble growth and the blend is gone
- Your neckline is starting to look fuzzy or undefined
- The top has grown out enough that the proportions of the cut feel off
- You've got an important event, interview, or occasion coming up

That last one is worth planning around. If you've got something big in 2 weeks, come in a few days before — not the day of, so the cut has time to settle.

## What to Do Between Cuts

A few habits that help your fade look fresher for longer:

**Moisturize your scalp.** Dry, flaky skin around a skin fade draws attention to itself. A lightweight scalp oil or moisturizer keeps the fade looking clean even as it grows out.

**Use a brush daily.** For textured or curly hair especially, a soft-bristle brush keeps the top portion of your fade laid down and defined between visits.

**Touch up your own edges if you can.** If you have a trimmer at home and know what you're doing, a quick neck cleanup can extend the freshness of your cut by several days. If you don't — don't risk it. Just book a touch-up.
