## TL;DR — the brutal truth

The site got **15 clicks in 90 days** from organic search. That's ~1 click every 6 days. The Google **Business Profile** (the map listing) is doing the heavy lifting — it's been seen for "auburn hair" 196 times in a month while the website was seen 4 times for the same term over 3 months. **The map listing is winning the local pack; the website is invisible behind it.**

**Why this matters:** The map listing converts on phone calls and direction clicks. The website converts on bookings and is the only place we can capture email, retarget visitors, or rank for non-local-pack searches (blog, services, neighborhoods). Right now we're leaving the website value on the table.

**The single biggest lever:** push the **17 mid-volume "near me" queries already ranking on page 2** up to page 1. They generated 374 impressions and 5 clicks last 90 days. At a normal page-1 CTR (5%), that same impression volume would yield ~19 clicks — a 4x lift with zero new content.

## What I'd skip

- **Generic single-word queries** ("hair", "haircut", "barber") — too competitive for a small local site. We rank top 5 already; chasing #1 means months of off-page SEO (backlinks). Not worth it for the volume.
- **Queries about other businesses** ("gene juarez salon", "maestro barbershop", "belle beauty braids") — these are people looking for competitors, not us. They show in GBP because Google maps clusters local results, but they're not our prospects.
- **Federal Way / Kent / other-city queries** — we have area pages for these but the impression volume is tiny (1–2 per query). Low priority until Auburn is dominated.
